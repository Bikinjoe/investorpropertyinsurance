The single most common investor insurance mistake is leaving a homeowner policy in place after a property becomes a rental. The policy looks fine on paper. The premium gets paid. Then a claim happens and the carrier denies it because the property was being used in a way the policy was not written for.

Here's the breakdown.

## What a homeowner policy assumes

A homeowner policy (HO-3 or HO-5) is built for an owner-occupied home. The carrier prices the policy assuming you live in the property. Buried in the policy language are conditions about occupancy, residence, and the use of the property. Renting it out long-term, short-term, or letting it sit vacant for an extended period can all violate those conditions.

When a claim happens on a rented-out homeowner policy, the adjuster comes out, sees evidence of rental activity (a tenant on site, a lease in your file, rental income in your tax return), and the carrier denies the claim or rescinds the policy.

## What a DP-3 actually does

A DP-3 (Dwelling Property 3) is built for rental properties. It's structurally similar to a homeowner policy but underwritten for the way investors use the property:

- Named insured is you as landlord, not as resident
- Open peril coverage on the dwelling
- Personal property coverage limited to items kept on site for tenant use (appliances, owner provided furnishings)
- Loss of rents instead of loss of use
- Landlord liability instead of personal liability
- Vacancy and tenant turnover treated as expected, not as exceptions

The premium difference between DP-3 and HO-3 is usually small. The claim difference at the moment something happens is large.

## When to switch

Three triggers should make you switch from homeowner to DP-3:

- The property becomes a rental (long-term tenant moves in)
- You start renting it short-term on Airbnb or VRBO (in this case you actually need an STR-specific policy, not a DP-3)
- You stop occupying the property and it sits vacant for an extended period

Don't assume your current carrier knows you rent the property because you mentioned it once on a phone call. The classification has to be on the policy declarations page.

## What to do today

If you have a property you rent out (long-term or short-term) and you're not sure what form the policy is on, pull the declarations page. Look for "policy form" or "policy type." If it says HO-3 or HO-5, you have a homeowner policy. Time to switch.
